## № 1: Software Development

There was a hype in the media trying to convince you that AIs can already do that or is at least relatively close to replacing programmers ([this](https://medium.com/can-ai-write-more-efficient-codes-than-humans/ai-in-coding-3bf234571d3c), also [this](https://medium.com/swlh/what-will-die-out-sooner-c-or-c-programmers-f4659dc243c0), [another one](https://medium.com/@kaushikshinde699/with-ai-writing-code-will-ai-replace-programmers-ce64d080c1f3)— also a little bit [this one](https://becominghuman.ai/10-artificial-intelligence-applications-that-can-generate-code-themselves-3eb9791c995e), and [that one](https://medium.com/@mshapir95/will-ai-replace-programmers-d9c58ddd536c)).

Believe me, if they could, this would spread like wildfire.

Software Engineers are among the most well-paid people. If they were not necessary, companies would not employ them. Especially not for that price. Companies like Google and Microsoft employ more than 250,000 people combined and many thousand developers. If there was even a tiny chance to get this done, they would put a lot of effort into it. This would not be big news in some weird blogs, but big news on all financial markets. We have millions of developers worldwide.

**What makes this task so hard?** Software describes processes. The simpler the process, the easier it is to write the software. As a software engineer, I have to have a good understanding of the problem and what people try to achieve. This is often not the same as what they say they want. Non-developers simply often lack the knowledge to precisely describe their issue. And everybody — including myself — has a hard time keeping the problem statement separated from the solution approach.

**Why would this be crazy?** AI that can improve AI in a non-trivial way. Singularity here we come!

**The footnote**: AI tools can, of course, support. Code autocompletion, question answering, creating docstring suggestions or suggestions for the text of pull requests, maybe even helping PMs to create more detailed tickets. All of this can make a developer way more efficient while not removing the need for a developer.

## № 2: Research

Figuring out how our world works is one of the hardest tasks of humanity. Here are some of the core research questions of the world:

* **Mathematics**: The 6 remaining [Millennium Prize problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Prize_Problems) give US\$1 million each to the person who solves it. They are all a bit hard to understand. If you are looking for something simpler: The [Collatz conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collatz_conjecture) is also not solved so far.
* **Computer Science**: [P vs NP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P_versus_NP_problem), Can the fast Fourier transform be computed in o(n log n) time? What is the fastest algorithm for matrix multiplication? There are many more [unsolved problems in computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_unsolved_problems_in_computer_science).
* **Physics**: There are lots of [unsolved problems in physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_unsolved_problems_in_physics).
* **Engineering**: Building a working fusion reactor.

The computer science and mathematics problems don’t require any real-world interactions and they have correct answers, although the verification might be pretty hard. For this reason, solving any of those would prove an impressive advancement of AI.

**What makes this task so hard?** At least for the mentioned examples it’s not applying a known pattern. It’s not a simple re-combination of things we know. A novel thought is needed. Having novel thoughts is way harder than re-combining existing work.

**Why would this be crazy?** Speeding up the pace at which humanity makes progress sounds amazing. Hopefully this would also mean that we make faster social policy improvements.

**The footnote**: While the listed problems are certainly not within the reach of our 2023 AI models, there also is research which is repetitive. Those might be in the reach of AI models.

## № 3: Creative Writing

Authors may not earn as much as software engineers, but the publishing industry is still a multi-billion-dollar market. Companies like [Penguin Random House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Random_House) and [HarperCollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HarperCollins) employ thousands of people. If AI could truly create novels with groundbreaking ideas, these companies would be all over it.

**What makes this task so difficult?** Storytelling involves more than just stringing words together. It’s about having a message you want to tell. Good stories have to have a thread that runs through the story, but also unexpected twists. Novel ideas that nobody else wrote down so far.

**Why would this be crazy?** Print publishing is a \$41 billion industry in the US in 2023 ([source](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/market-size/book-publishing-united-states/#:~:text=Questions%20Clients%20Ask%20About%20This%20Industry&text=The%20market%20size%2C%20measured%20by,decline%20%2D0.1%25%20in%202023.)) with over 800,000 employees ([source](https://www.statista.com/statistics/184360/employment-in-us-publishing-industries-since-2001/#:~:text=In%20January%202022%2C%20a%20total,January%20of%20the%20previous%20year.)). Most of those could be replaced by a single program if it was that good. Hell, if it manages to get the plot right it can even generate custom books on the fly tailored to your needs on that specific day.

**The footnote**: AI tools can indeed be helpful in certain aspects of writing. They can assist with grammar and spelling, offer suggestions for synonyms, or even help generate ideas for new storylines. They can certainly also create new stories — they are just not so interesting.

However, these applications merely supplement the creative process, rather than replacing the need for a human author with a distinct voice and vision. If you want to see the current state, go to [reddit.com/r/WritingPrompts](https://www.reddit.com/r/WritingPrompts/new/). There you can find a small idea for a story. Give it to an AI like this:

> Write an interesting or exciting story along this idea: [the writing prompt]

## № 4: The last mile in package delivery

Machines help a lot in sorting letters and packages. Algorithms can compute optimal routes and the best locations for distribution centers. It turns out that the simple task of putting the letter in your postbox or bringing the package to your front door is a lot harder to automatize.

**What makes this task so hard?** There are tons of variations in the design of post boxes, the path to the post box, and its location. The package delivery guy needs to interact with people, needs to decide if they can give the package to a neighbor. The package itself might be damaged or open. Maybe something is leaking. There might not be a good parking spot for the large truck.

**Why would this be crazy?** Over a million people in the US work in package delivery ([source](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1173810/couriers-local-delivery-services-market-employees-number-us/)) with a market size of \$157 billion ([source](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/market-size/couriers-local-delivery-services-united-states/)). No wonder Amazon is trying to use drones.

**No footnote here**. AI simply cannot do it right now. Even if we try to avoid issues like with drone delivery. It’s still not a solved task. Not even close.

## № 5: Actually most jobs

Tool usage is the main blocker for large language models (LLMs) to fly. Most white-collar jobs need to read e-mails, based on that information interact with Excel files / PDF documents / domain-specific websites. As soon as this is given, we will live in a crazy world.

Let’s go through it:

1. **Read e-mails**: The e-mail could just be a prompt to a LLM. Something like “An e-mail follows. What should be done?: [the email]”
1. **Read PDF documents**: That is similar to reading the e-mail. However, I’m not sure if LLMs can deal with pixel-images as input in a reasonable way. If the PDF contains any diagrams, that would be necessary. Except for that, a PDF text extraction library like pypdf can be used.
1. **Read and manipulate spreadsheets (Excel files)**: Here we have a large gap for which I don’t know how we can close it. It feels like every business I have ever seen has at least one crucial spreadsheet. If an LLM could access a spreadsheet, it could get recent data. That would make it so much more powerful. Sadly, I think the amount of publicly available business-relevant spreadsheets is rather limited.
