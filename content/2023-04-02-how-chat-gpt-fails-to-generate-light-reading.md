We all want some forms of light entertainment once in a while. It might be to stop us from thinking about work at the end of the day. It might be to distract from hardships in our lives. It might just be to relax.

Light reading could be love stories ([Rosamunde Pilcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosamunde_Pilcher)), fantasy, science-fiction ([Douglas Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Adams)), crime novels, …

The stories are often very similar, only the details and the universe in which they happen varies. Especially for short-stories, the thought that GPT-4 could generate something similar is not far-fetched. I gave it a shot with GPT-4. Let me summarize some observations.

## №1: Too Brief and Descriptive

Chat-GPT is often too brief and descriptive. For example, for Lord of the Rings, it might simply write

> Frodo and Sam, guided by Gollum, trudge through the treacherous and barren lands of Mordor, avoiding Sauron’s watchful gaze. They endure countless hardships and rely on their unyielding determination to reach Mount Doom, where the One Ring must be destroyed.

instead of letting the reader actually participate in the “countless hardships”. Due to the brevity, the reader is emotionally detached.

## №2: Missing Back references

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/KAmIHa-Rztg" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen></iframe>

It is impressive that Chat GPT can follow a consistent thread over hundreds of words. However, when good human authors write a lot, they add back references. Just minor details that are present in multiple parts of the story. Ice Age does this with the squirrel Scrat. Scrat is not important to the story, but a detail that people recognize. Such might even have an important meaning — which leads me to another shortcoming of Chat GPT.

## №3: Missing revelations — no spoilers necessary

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/UP8NRgWtPaE" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen></iframe>

I absolutely love [heist films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heist_film) like [Ocean’s Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean%27s_Eleven), [Inception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inception), or [Now You See Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Now_You_See_Me_%28film%29). The core element of those movies is a revelation at the end of the story which completely changes how you view the story. The trailers above show the concept pretty well.

It’s funny and interesting, because it’s unexpected.

While you can tell Chat GPT explicitly to add a concrete revelation, I haven’t managed to make it add a revelation on its own. And it keeps spoiling the story.

## №4: Missing Character Growth

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/SwfmV3nn6QA" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen></iframe>

We love stories of underdogs. Harry Potter, the poor orphan who turns out to be an excellent wizard. [The Pursuit of Happyness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pursuit_of_Happyness). [Moneyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyball). [Real Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Steel).

A crucial part of the underdog experience is to see the suffering. We want to see the growth. And to see it, we have to feel the suffering beforehand.

## №5: Suspense and surprise

Modern series have mastered the cliffhanger to ensure people continue watching. While it’s too much in many movies and series today, the stories generated by ChatGPT lack the suspense and surprise.

## Conclusion

ChatGPT (GPT-4) cannot write interesting stories. But you might be able to use it as a tool for parts of your novel. I’m not a novel writer, but I gave it a shot:
