Down Jackets

It's getting below 0 °C in Munich again and I'm looking for clothes for Nepal. So it is about time to get a good jacket. In this article, I'll have a look at down jackets. Down jackets are a second layer.

The three layers are:

  1. Underwear: A T-Shirt or something similar that is directly on your skin. Merino wool, microfibre or mixtures are common choices.
  2. Insulation and moisture transport: Down jacket or fleece for keeping you warm.
  3. Weather Layer: Keeping wind / rain away. A softshell or a hardshell, depending on the climate.

One of their quality measures is the fill power. It measures the fluffiness in cuin (cubic inches per ounce). The fluffier, the more insulating a given amount is. One online source (Globetrotter) recommended 500-700 cuin for everyday purposes and 650-900 cuin for expeditions and very cold temperatures.

Another quality measure of down filling is the mixture (down / feathers). The same Globetrotter YouTube video recommended 80/20 for everyday usage and 96/4 or 95/5 for sports.

A synthetic alternative to down is PrimaLoft. It comes in 3 variants: Gold, Silver, Black, where Gold is the best and Black is the worst. The weight per square meter determines how warm it is. 100 g/m² is for normal insulation jackets; 200 g / m² is for super warm winter jackets.

Then have a look at the sewing. Whether it is sewn through ("durchgesteppt") or has box baffles ("Boxkammer") might make a difference.

Some jackets can be packed really well, as the chest pocket doubles as a stuff sack.

Features

  • Buttons to close: nice for ventilation and as an alternative in case the zipper breaks
  • Pockets: Front-hip, front-chest, inside - especially the inside one might be important for water or electronic devices!
  • Drawcords: At the bottom of the jacket to prevent cold air from coming in
  • Elastic part at the hand to make sure it closes well

Overview

The ones with green background color were recommended for Nepal.

Self-Stowing Pocket = Self-pocketing: Some jackets come with an inner pocket into which the jacket packs itself. Some, like the Arc'teryx Jackets, are meant to be self-pocketed into the arms.
